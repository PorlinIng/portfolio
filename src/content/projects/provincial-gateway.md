**CSA Design Competition — 1st & 2nd Award Winner, 2016**

**Provincial Gate Way to Cambodia Heritage** is a CSA Design Competition entry proposing a series of gateway structures along Cambodia's National Roads — the connective tissue between the country's 25 cities and provinces. The scheme responds to a lack of local identity at province boundaries and aims to redefine those thresholds as meaningful places of arrival, rest, and community.

**Concept**
The design draws from the Hindu-Khmer creation myth *Churning the Ocean of Milk* (Samudra Manthan) — the act of churning the cosmic ocean with devotion and collective effort. This mythology, depicted on the walls of Angkor Wat and still central to Khmer culture, frames the gateway as a place of shared purpose: welcoming travellers, celebrating identity, and fostering community.

The built form synthesises four elements drawn from the Cambodian landscape and culture:
- **Khmer Greeting Form** — the Som Peah gesture, translated into the bridge's sweeping arch as a gesture of welcome
- **Rice Field** — the horizontal datum and green landscape threading through the site
- **Naga** — the serpent deity, giving flow and dynamism to the structural form
- **Khmer Temple Form** — the massing of the community buildings, recalling temple enclosure geometry

**Site & Programme**
Sites along Cambodia's National Roads, land allocation 50m × 100m on both sides of the road (130m total). The programme includes:
- Sky Walk Way — a cable-stayed pedestrian bridge spanning the road, the gateway's iconic element
- Green Parks and landscaped rest areas
- Community Center (E0 = 520m², E1 = 650m²) — outdoor performance space, gallery, modern shop, local food market
- Canteen (E0 = 520m², E1 = 650m²) — food and beverage for travellers
- Parking areas for buses, cars, and motorbikes

**Physical Model**
A detailed physical scale model was produced and exhibited at the competition — demonstrating the spatial relationships of the gateway components, the landscape strategy, and the structural logic of the bridge form.
