**PPCC Street Furniture Design Competition — 2nd Award Winner, 2019**

A competition entry for urban public furniture in Phnom Penh, presenting an innovative street station design drawn from Khmer material culture.

**Palm Hat Station**
The design takes its form and concept from the traditional **Khmer Palm Hat** — a woven hat worn by people across Cambodia as protection from sun and rain, and a symbol of rest, nature, and simplicity. The pavilion translates this everyday object into a piece of public infrastructure: a circular timber-and-steel canopy structure that provides shade and shelter while serving three programmatic functions:

- **Mobile Phone Station** — integrated charging points for public use
- **Relaxing Place** — seating around the perimeter
- **Information Board** — display surface for community and city information

**Dimensions:** R = 3m, H = 2.5m

**Flexibility**
The Palm Hat Station is designed as a modular system. The full circle can be deployed in large public spaces, or cut to 1/2 or 1/3 of the circle for narrower park paths and street edges — making a single design adaptable to a wide range of urban conditions without redesign.

**Design Analysis**
The canopy structure is composed of radial timber ribs splaying outward from a central column, referencing the woven structure of the palm hat itself. Views in plan show the shadow cast in the afternoon of November 21st — demonstrating the design's solar performance as well as its visual character from above.

The project was exhibited at the PPCC Street Furniture Design competition and awarded 2nd place.
